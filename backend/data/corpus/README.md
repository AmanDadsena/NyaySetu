# Ingested corpus

JSONL files here are generated by `python -m app.rag.ingest` and merged into the
retrieval index at startup. One file per source, one passage per line.

They are committed so a deploy does not need to re-ingest, and so a change to
the legal corpus shows up as a reviewable diff rather than happening silently
inside a running service.

## Adding a source

    python -m app.rag.ingest bare-act data/raw/bns.txt \
        --name bns_2023 \
        --act "Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023" \
        --url "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/20062"

    python -m app.rag.eval        # confirm nothing regressed
    python -m app.rag.ingest list

## What belongs here

Material that is free to reproduce:

- **Bare acts** from indiacode.nic.in. Section 52(1)(q) of the Copyright Act,
  1957 permits reproduction of any Act of a legislature and of any judicial
  pronouncement.
- **Judgments** from the Supreme Court and High Court portals and eCourts.
- **Government guidance** from NALSA, consumerhelpline, parivahan, RTI portals.

## What does not

Legal textbooks, commentaries and digests — Ratanlal & Dhirajlal, Mulla, SCC
headnotes — are copyrighted. Ingesting them would expose the project to an
infringement claim, which is a poor look for a legal-aid tool.

They are also the wrong input. A commentary is a scholar's argument *about* a
provision; the provision itself is the authority, and it is free. An assistant
that quotes a commentary as though it were the law is more dangerous than one
that says less.

## Recalibrate after a large ingest

`MIN_ABSOLUTE_BM25` in `retriever.py` is the floor below which the assistant
declines to answer. Adding thousands of passages changes the idf distribution
and therefore the scale of raw scores. Re-run `python -m app.rag.eval` and check
the false-positive count is still zero before shipping.
